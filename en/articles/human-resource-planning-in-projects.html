Human Resource Planning in Projects: Building High-Performing Teams

By Ashraf Ibrahim El Desoky · Jul 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Introduction

Human resource planning is the foundation of project success. Without the right people in the right roles, even the best-planned projects will struggle. Effective HR planning ensures that project teams are assembled efficiently, developed continuously, and managed to achieve peak performance.

The HR Planning Process

1. Define Resource Requirements

Identify the roles, skills, and number of team members needed. Create a resource breakdown structure that maps required competencies to project activities.

2. Create the Staffing Management Plan

Document how and when project team members will be acquired, released, and managed. Include:

  • Resource acquisition strategy (internal vs. external)
  • Timeline for staffing
  • Training and development plans
  • Recognition and reward systems
  • Compliance with labor regulations

3. Develop a Team Charter

A team charter establishes shared understanding of:

  • Team values and working agreements
  • Communication guidelines
  • Decision-making processes
  • Conflict resolution procedures
  • Meeting norms

Acquiring Team Members

Internal Staffing

  • Negotiate with functional managers for resource allocation
  • Consider existing employee skills and career development goals
  • Use resource calendars to check availability

External Staffing

  • Procure contractors or consultants for specialized skills
  • Use staffing agencies for temporary resources
  • Conduct interviews and skills assessments

Virtual Teams

  • Leverage global talent pools
  • Establish communication protocols for different time zones
  • Use collaboration tools effectively

Developing the Team

Training and Development

  • Identify skill gaps through competency assessments
  • Provide technical, leadership, and soft skills training
  • Encourage certifications and professional development
  • Create mentoring relationships

Team Building

  • Conduct regular team-building activities
  • Foster psychological safety for open communication
  • Celebrate milestones and achievements
  • Build trust through transparency and consistency

Retaining Team Members

  • Provide meaningful work and clear roles
  • Offer competitive compensation and benefits
  • Create growth opportunities within the project
  • Recognize and reward contributions publicly
  • Conduct stay interviews to understand engagement

Measuring Team Performance

  • Track individual and team productivity metrics
  • Monitor team satisfaction through regular surveys
  • Assess quality of deliverables
  • Evaluate collaboration effectiveness
  • Review turnover and retention rates

Conclusion

Human resource planning is not a one-time activity—it requires continuous attention throughout the project lifecycle. By investing in team acquisition, development, and retention, project managers build high-performing teams that deliver exceptional results.
